# MemberAssist Member FAQ

> **Important:** This FAQ gives general information about U.S. health insurance. Your plan documents and denial notice control the details for your plan. Deadlines and processes can vary by plan and state. MemberAssist is a demonstration chatbot that uses synthetic data; it is not your insurer and does not make coverage decisions.

## 1. How do I file a health insurance claim?

- A claim is a request for your health plan to cover or pay for health care.
- Your health care provider will usually file the claim for you.
- If you paid for care yourself or your provider tells you to file a claim, check your plan’s instructions or contact your insurer for the correct form, deadline, and supporting documents.
- Keep copies of bills, receipts, claim forms, and messages about the claim.
- Review your Explanation of Benefits (EOB). It explains how your plan processed the claim. An EOB is not necessarily a bill.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/explanation-of-benefits/

## 2. What should I do if my claim is denied?

- Read the denial notice and your EOB.
- Check the reason for the denial and the instructions for challenging it.
- Contact your insurer if the reason is unclear. Ask what information or documents are needed.
- Keep copies of the denial notice, EOB, medical records or letters you choose to submit, and your communications.
- You may be able to appeal if you believe the plan should cover the service or pay the claim.
- Do not assume that every denial can be overturned. The result depends on the facts, plan terms, and applicable rules.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/

## 3. How do internal appeals work?

An internal appeal asks your health insurance company to review its decision again.

1. Follow the appeal instructions in your denial notice.
2. Complete the insurer’s required form, or write to the insurer if its instructions allow that.
3. Include your name, claim number, health insurance ID number, and supporting information. A letter from your clinician may help explain the medical facts.
4. Keep copies of everything you send and note the dates of calls and messages.

**Deadline:** HealthCare.gov says you generally must file an internal appeal within **180 days (6 months)** of receiving notice that your claim was denied. Check your denial notice and plan rules to confirm the deadline that applies to you.

**Urgent or expedited appeals:** If waiting for the standard process could seriously jeopardize your life or your ability to regain maximum function, ask for an expedited appeal. Explain why the situation is urgent. In urgent situations, you may be able to request an external review before finishing the internal appeal, and you may be able to request both at the same time. Contact your insurer immediately for instructions.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/

## 4. What is an external review, and when can I request one?

An external review means an independent reviewer—not your health plan—reviews certain insurance decisions.

- You may be able to request external review after your insurer’s internal appeal still denies the claim.
- Certain denials involving medical judgment, experimental or investigational treatment, or cancellation of coverage may qualify.
- In an urgent situation, you may be able to request external review before the internal appeal process is complete.
- HealthCare.gov says a written request generally must be filed within **4 months** after you receive the insurer’s denial notice or final determination. Check your notice and applicable state or plan process.
- The external reviewer may uphold the denial or decide in your favor. The insurer must accept the external reviewer’s decision under the applicable process.
- Check the final denial notice or EOB for instructions and contact details.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/external-review/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/external-review/

## 5. What are the typical timelines?

The timelines below are the general timelines listed by HealthCare.gov. Your situation, plan, and applicable state process may affect the steps or deadline.

| Step | General timeline listed by HealthCare.gov |
|---|---|
| Insurer responds to a prior-authorization request | Within 15 days |
| Insurer responds about medical services already received | Within 30 days |
| Insurer responds in an urgent-care case | Within 72 hours |
| Deadline to file an internal appeal | Within 180 days (6 months) after receiving the denial notice |
| Internal appeal decision for a service not yet received | Within 30 days |
| Internal appeal decision for a service already received | Within 60 days |
| Deadline to request external review | Within 4 months after the denial notice or final determination |
| Standard external review decision | As soon as possible, no later than 45 days after the request |
| Expedited external review decision | As soon as possible, no later than 72 hours after the request |

These are not guarantees for every plan or every state. Read your notices carefully. If the matter is urgent, contact your insurer promptly and ask about expedited review.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/external-review/

## 6. How can I reach a person? When does MemberAssist hand me to an agent?

- In this **MemberAssist demo**, ask for a human agent if you need help with a complicated denial, an appeal, an urgent case, or a question the chatbot cannot answer confidently.
- The demo may hand the conversation to an agent when you request one, when the issue needs human review, or when the chatbot cannot safely resolve the request.
- MemberAssist cannot promise that an agent is available or give a real insurer’s phone number. **VERIFY the real insurer’s contact details** using your insurance card, plan documents, or denial notice.
- If you need help with an insurance problem, HealthCare.gov says your state’s Consumer Assistance Program or Department of Insurance may be able to help.
- For questions about HealthCare.gov or Marketplace coverage, use the official HealthCare.gov contact page.

**Official source URLs**
- https://www.healthcare.gov/how-can-i-get-consumer-help-if-i-have-insurance/
- https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/

*Note: MemberAssist’s handoff behavior is a project/demo design choice, not a rule established by HealthCare.gov.*

## 7. What can and cannot MemberAssist do?

**MemberAssist can:**
- Explain general health insurance terms and the information available in the demo plan documents.
- Help you understand the synthetic claim status and denial reason shown in the demo.
- Explain a cost estimate when the required plan information is available.
- Point out when information is missing and suggest that you contact the insurer or a human agent.

**MemberAssist cannot:**
- Give medical advice, diagnose a condition, recommend treatment, or provide medication doses.
- Replace your clinician, insurer, official plan documents, or formal appeal process.
- Guarantee that a service is covered, a claim will be paid, or an appeal will succeed.
- Access or disclose another member’s private claim information.
- Treat demo records as real member records.

**This is a demo:** MemberAssist uses synthetic/fake member, plan, and claim data. It does not represent a real member account or make real insurance decisions. For urgent medical concerns, contact a qualified health professional or emergency services as appropriate.
